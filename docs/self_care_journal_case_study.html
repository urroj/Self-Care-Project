01 — PROBLEM

Your most personal data deserves tools you own

Most wellness and finance apps share a common flaw: they are not really tools for you — they are data collection pipelines dressed as tools. Your cycle patterns, mood records, and investment behaviour are the product. They leave the moment you open the app.

At the same time, the most meaningful dimensions of wellbeing — physical health, mental clarity, daily structure, and financial stability — are almost never addressed in one place. You carry four different apps, each knowing only a fragment of the picture.

Commercial health apps monetise sensitive cycle, mood, and symptom data — often sharing it with third parties without meaningful consent.

Generic cycle models built on regular-cycle averages produce wide, unreliable predictions for anyone with high variability.

Finance apps hand you charts without teaching you how the forecasts are made — encouraging passive consumption over mindful investing.

No privacy-respecting tool links physical health, daily habits, reflective journaling, and financial tracking in a single local application.

02 — PHILOSOPHY

Health, structure, and stability are one system

This project began as a menstrual cycle tracker for irregular cycles — a practical ML problem worth solving. But the further the build went, the clearer the underlying insight became: physical health does not exist in isolation. Mental and physical wellbeing are tightly linked to how organised and calm your daily life feels, and that feeling is inseparable from financial security.

Rather than building four separate apps, the design follows a single intentional progression.

03 — CYCLE ML

A three-phase model that learns you, not the average

The core insight: prediction confidence should grow as the model accumulates personal data, transitioning through mathematically distinct regimes rather than applying a one-size-fits-all model from day one.

PHASE 1
COLD START
< 3 CYCLES
Negative Binomial Population Prior + GradientBoosting

Bootstrapped from two peer-reviewed public datasets (Fehring/Marquette 2012 and Physionet mcPHASES 2024), filtered to the irregular-cycle subset. NB distribution was chosen over Gaussian because cycle length is a positive integer with right-skew overdispersion — a Gaussian prior would systematically underestimate the upper tail, exactly where irregular cycles live. CI: ±10–14 days.

PHASE 2
BAYESIAN
3–7 CYCLES
Conjugate Gamma-Poisson Bayesian Update

Each completed cycle shifts the posterior toward the user's personal pattern. Personal weight grows from 37% at 3 cycles to 87% at 7 cycles — a principled transition from population knowledge to individual truth. The conjugate form means updates are closed-form and computationally trivial. CI narrows to ±4–7 days.

PHASE 3
LSTM
8+ CYCLES
Two-layer stacked LSTM + Monte Carlo Dropout

Pre-trained on public sequences and fine-tuned on personal data at 0.2× learning rate to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Inference runs 100 stochastic forward passes for a full predictive distribution rather than a single point estimate. 6-cycle lookback window. Huber loss (δ=2.0) for robustness to outlier cycles. CI narrows to ±2–4 days.

DATA
LAYER
Local PostgreSQL — no cloud, no third parties

All cycle records, daily logs, journal entries, habits, and investment data live in a local PostgreSQL instance. The only external calls are read-only market price requests to Yahoo Finance via a FastAPI proxy — no personal data ever leaves the machine.

04 — ETF TRACKER MODULE

A production-grade Islamic ETF forecasting pipeline

The ETF module is not a wrapper around a charting library. It is a multi-layer financial forecasting pipeline built from first principles — model selection, volatility estimation, market-regime detection, Shariah-specific signal engineering, and honest walk-forward validation, all running locally and serving a React frontend via FastAPI.

Four layers of the forecast

Holt-Winters ETS (1Y / 5Y) + Log-linear OLS (10Y)

Horizon selection drives model choice. Short and medium horizons use Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing — undamped for 1Y (52 weekly steps), damped for 5Y (260 steps) to prevent over-extrapolation. The 10-year horizon switches to log-linear OLS on monthly bars, fitting log(y) = a + b·t and exponentiating back, with CAGR and R² as primary metrics.

GARCH(1,1) + ETS residual floor → blended CI

The 95% confidence band is not taken from ETS alone. A GARCH(1,1) model fits log-return conditional variance and forecasts per-step uncertainty. The final band uses an element-wise max of the GARCH cumulative price std and the ETS residual floor (σ·√h), then scales the result by the HMM regime multiplier. This means the CI is wide when markets are volatile and the regime is stressed — not a fixed ±% figure.

2-state HMM → CI multiplier (1.0× or 1.35×)

A Hidden Markov Model with Gaussian emissions is fitted on ETF log returns joined with VIX data. The two latent states correspond to low-volatility and high-volatility market regimes. In the high-vol state the CI multiplier is set to 1.35, widening both bands to reflect elevated uncertainty. The current regime, transition probabilities, and state label are surfaced to the user in the diagnostics panel.

Islamic index rebalance flags + XLK/XLF ratio

Shariah ETFs face periodic rebalance pressure during MSCI Islamic (Feb/May/Aug/Nov) and DJIM (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) review weeks (days 8–24 of the review month). These weeks are flagged as binary features and collapsed into a rebalance_pressure_pct signal. Separately, the XLK/XLF ratio (tech vs financials sector relative strength) is computed — Shariah ETFs systematically overweight tech and exclude financials, so this ratio is a domain-relevant leading indicator. Rolling 26-week correlations vs SPY and VIX complete the enrichment layer.

Exogenous context series

The pipeline pulls four external series at the same bar frequency as the ETF: ^VIX (market fear gauge), XLK (tech sector), XLF (financials sector), and SPY (broad market). These feed the HMM, the factor regression, and the Shariah feature layer. Every exogenous series is optional — if Yahoo Finance is unreachable or rate-limited, the pipeline degrades key by key rather than failing.

Factor regression

An OLS regression of ETF log returns on SPY and XLK log returns produces three outputs that matter for Islamic investors: Jensen's alpha (annualised excess return independent of market exposure), βmarket (sensitivity to broad market moves), and βtech (technology sector tilt). Tracking error and R² round out the attribution picture. These numbers answer the question a mindful investor actually needs to ask: is this fund generating independent return, or is it just leveraged SPY?

Walk-forward validation — the honesty layer

Every forecast is validated with an expanding-window walk-forward evaluation. The validation horizon is matched to the forecast horizon where data permits, and a wf_capped flag is set if it had to be shortened — the UI never claims more validation than the data supports.

RMSE / MAE
Price-space prediction error across up to 20 out-of-sample folds
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error — scale-independent across different-priced ETFs
DIR. ACCURACY
% of folds where the model predicted the correct direction vs the previous close
CI COVERAGE
% of actuals that fell inside the forecast's 95% confidence band
CRPS
Continuous Ranked Probability Score — measures both calibration and sharpness of the probabilistic forecast
WF SHARPE
Annualised Sharpe of a long-if-forecast-positive / short-otherwise strategy across folds

Graceful degradation

GARCH requires arch · HMM requires hmmlearn · CRPS exact form requires scipy · Exogenous series require a live network connection. Every optional component has a documented fallback: missing GARCH → ETS residual σ floor only. Missing HMM → ci_multiplier fixed at 1.0. Missing exogenous → Shariah features, factor regression, and VIX-informed HMM degrade key by key. The only hard prerequisites are a valid symbol and enough bars (≥ 20 weekly for ETS, ≥ 12 monthly for OLS).

Portfolio tracker

Alongside forecasting, the module tracks actual investments. Adding an entry records the closing price on the purchase date (fetched from Yahoo Finance at insert time), computes units held, and from that point forward calculates current value, gain/loss, and return % against live prices. A cumulative Invested vs Current Value chart shows portfolio trajectory over time. All records are stored in PostgreSQL — no spreadsheet, no cloud.

08 — REFLECTION

Key engineering decisions

Choosing Negative Binomial over Gaussian for Phase 1 was the first meaningful technical decision: cycle lengths are integer-valued and right-skewed, so a Gaussian prior would systematically underestimate the upper tail — exactly where irregular cycles live.

Fine-tuning the LSTM at 0.2× learning rate with Huber loss guards against catastrophic forgetting and single-cycle outliers, letting the model stay flexible without being destabilised by noise.

The blended CI approach in the ETF pipeline — taking the element-wise max of GARCH cumulative variance and ETS residual floor, then scaling by the HMM multiplier — avoids the failure mode where a point-forecast model reports confidently narrow bands during historically stressed markets. Each layer adds a floor, not a ceiling.

Building the walk-forward validator to cap its own horizon when data is insufficient, and surfacing the wf_capped flag to the UI, was a deliberate choice: a forecast tool that tells you when it cannot validate itself honestly is more trustworthy than one that silently reports metrics computed on too few folds.

Using raw psycopg2 with plain SQL instead of an ORM keeps every database operation fully auditable. Using PyWebView instead of Electron keeps the binary footprint small and removes Node.js as a production runtime dependency — in deployment, the React build is static files served by FastAPI, so there is exactly one process to manage.
